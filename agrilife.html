Case study 01 · Consulting / Process analysis

Designing an ITSM-aligned demand generation framework.

A process-analysis engagement for a university technology service center—moving from informal outreach practices to a documented blueprint for tracking demand, encouraging adoption, and supporting consistent follow-up.

01 / The context

Start with the actual problem.

Texas A&M AgriLife's Center for Managed Technology Services (CMTS) provides technology services to researchers and faculty. At the start of the project, the center lacked a formal, documented demand generation framework for organizing outreach, service inquiries, and longer-term engagement.

The five-person capstone consulting team was asked to document the current situation and design a Version 1.0 process that CMTS could evaluate for future adoption, while working within existing Texas A&M systems and security constraints.

Project objective: Build a repeatable, measurable demand generation process—not a live software deployment. The original project charter explicitly excluded implementing a production platform or managing ongoing outreach.

02 / My contribution

Model the work before changing it.

I served as the team's Process Analyst, with primary responsibility for transforming information from stakeholder discussions and project research into understandable business process documentation.

  • Created an As-Is process model showing how outreach channels, service requests, consultations, and decisions connected in the current workflow.
  • Developed a To-Be process model illustrating opportunities for standardized intake, tracking, approvals, delivery, and feedback.
  • Contributed process and value-stream analysis to help the team describe how engagement could progress from initial awareness to continued service adoption.
  • Worked with teammates to align the proposed workflow with the recommended Microsoft 365 / Power Platform approach.

As-Is: Current state

Informal outreach channels flowed toward service inquiries, consultation, and a service decision, with limited formal tracking between stages.

Outreach→Inquiry→Consultation→Decision

To-Be: Proposed process

A structured future-state flow introduced lead capture, service workflow checkpoints, and a feedback loop.

Outreach→Capture→Triage→Service→Feedback

03 / Solution design

A framework for measurable engagement.

The team's proposed design connected the demand lifecycle to clearer handoffs and reporting opportunities. A structured funnel was used to organize different engagement stages and identify where the center might monitor effectiveness.

04 / Delivery

What we actually delivered.

The capstone concluded with documented deliverables and project closure. The team presented recommendations and provided a blueprint that the center could consider for later implementation.

Design artifacts

Current-state and future-state process diagrams, demand generation funnel, process/value analysis, and recommended improvements.

Delivery artifacts

Project charter, stakeholder documentation, implementation guidance, KPI measurement framework, final presentation, and closing documentation.

Outcome: Version 1.0 framework and supporting documentation delivered within the project's six-week schedule. The source documents do not establish that a CRM was deployed or that adoption and retention metrics improved afterward.

What I learned

Process improvement begins with careful discovery. It is easy to jump to a technology solution, but modeling the existing workflow first made proposed changes easier to explain, evaluate, and communicate to stakeholders. The engagement also reinforced the importance of clear scope boundaries and realistic implementation assumptions.
