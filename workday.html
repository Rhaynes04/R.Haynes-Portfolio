Evaluating a Workday Collections migration proof of concept.

Supporting an enterprise team's decision-making by comparing legacy collections workflows with Workday functionality, identifying scope differences, and documenting test scenarios and reporting considerations.

01 / The context

Can a new system support the way teams work?

A technology implementation can only succeed when the target system supports the business processes people depend on. During my internship, I supported a proof-of-concept assessment for transitioning collections activities from a legacy GetPaid environment to Workday Collections.

The evaluation required understanding existing workflows and checking how they translated into available Workday functionality, especially across communications, account assignment, follow-up processes, escalation, and reporting.

My task: Help turn existing operational needs into traceable requirements and practical test scenarios so the team could evaluate feasibility and make better-informed implementation decisions.
02 / My approach

Translate workflows into testable questions.

My work focused on examining the current state, evaluating the target system, and presenting findings clearly enough for stakeholders to use during a decision process.

01 / Understand the legacy process

Reviewed GetPaid workflows and gathered requirements related to reports, customer communications, account ownership, and collections controls.

02 / Compare target functionality

Validated Workday capabilities against current-state needs and surfaced a scope shift the team had not previously identified.

03 / Define test scenarios

Developed four UAT scenarios and decision documentation for workflow validation and feasibility review.

04 / Review reporting

Evaluated dashboard and report concepts covering customer balances, aging, risk, and collections activity.

What mattered most

Discovering a scope difference early can change the direction of implementation planning. This experience taught me that requirements validation is not just an administrative task—it is an essential part of managing project risk and keeping technical decisions grounded in operational reality.

Scope of result: I contributed requirements analysis, test scenarios, reporting reviews, and feasibility documentation. This case study does not claim ownership of the broader migration, completion of production implementation, or any unverified financial benefits.
